## Learner and outcomes

Written for Chris as a general commercial-product assessor. No legal qualification or completion of another course is assumed. Basic reading of a product description is assumed; essential AI Act vocabulary is retaught.

After this course the learner should be able to:

1. Define the assessed system, intended purpose, actor and applicable high-risk route.
2. Select risk controls and testing evidence appropriate to that purpose.
3. Assess data quality and distinguish lawful exceptional bias processing from unrestricted sensitive-data use.
4. Specify documentation, logging, instructions, oversight and performance evidence.
5. Allocate duties across provider, deployer, representative, importer and distributor, including changed roles.
6. Decide whether deployment needs additional notices, impact assessments or a specialist biometric route.
7. Assemble a reasoned evidence dossier with unresolved facts, dates, owners and escalation points.

## Scope and boundary

Primary teaching targets are **Articles 4a and 8–27**, with necessary bridges to Articles 2–6, 111 and 113 and Annexes I, III and IV. This course introduces the provider's conformity, declaration, marking and registration duties as release gates; the assessment procedures and standards receive their own later course. Likewise, post-market monitoring and serious-incident reporting are introduced here through Articles 9, 17, 20 and 26; detailed Articles 72–73 workflows belong in the operations course.

The baseline is Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, consolidated on 27 July 2026. Chapter III Sections 1–3 have separate Annex III and Annex I dates, with Article 111 transitions; a classification and a presently applicable duty are different findings. See the [Commission framework page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) and the precise statute references in the coverage register.

The [Commission high-risk classification page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-high-risk-systems) still identifies its guidelines as **draft** when checked on 7 October 2026. Those examples will not be treated as binding law or evidence of final adoption.

## Teaching cases

**Northbridge Hiring** is a fictional supplier of an AI candidate-ranking system sold to employers. The exercise stipulates an Annex III point 4(a) intended purpose, material influence on shortlisting and no established Article 6(3) exception. It is a teaching case, not a classification of a named real supplier. Provider and employer deployer receive separate responsibilities. No fictional test number will be presented as a statutory threshold.

Each module advances its dossier: define scope; assess data; specify technical controls; allocate organisational duties; decide deployment readiness. Later examples separately stipulate a public-service deployer or an Annex III point 5(b)/(c) use to test Article 27. A private recruitment employer is not automatically subject to that provision.

The real Flashkarte case provides a useful comparison: external AI authoring, stored teaching material and rule-based quiz grading need separate boundaries. We will revisit it if institutional AI assessment, learner profiling or a hosted agent is added. It must not be silently converted into a high-risk example just because the course concerns education.
