# Proposed provision coverage and sources

**Status: planned, not taught. Checked 7 October 2026.** These rows define what later lessons must teach and test. Merely listing an article is not evidence that it has been taught. The module case exercises test selected applications, not every paragraph in one question.

## Sources and reading limits

- **AI-C-EN:** [27 July 2026 English consolidation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), retained in [the source folder](../sources/ai-act-2026-07-27-en.pdf). Articles 4a and 8–27 and Annex IV were read in full for this proposal; Articles 111–113 and Annex III point 3 were checked for the date and real-product branches. Other bridge provisions use the existing programme's recorded source review. The consolidation is a documentation reference; the Official Journal acts govern.
- **OJ baseline:** [original Regulation 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng) and [amending Regulation 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32026R1744). See the [programme source register](../source-register.md) for retained files, hashes and earlier reading limits. No full reading of every original recital, sector instrument or national law is claimed.
- **Commission status:** [AI Act framework](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) confirms the amendment and separate high-risk dates. The [high-risk guideline page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-high-risk-systems) still describes classification guidance as draft. Website summaries cannot replace the exact statute, especially its limited applicability and transitions.
- **Real-product evidence:** [Flashkarte case record](../case-studies/flashkarte-ai-authoring.md) cites inspected implementation and documents. It is a local source review, not a live production, contractual or customer-use audit.

Before authoring, refresh the cited legal instruments and interpretation status. Read any new sector, GDPR, national procedure or technical standard source used in a screen in full at the relevant provision level; preserve its authority and limits. This package does not claim current harmonised standards or simplified forms have been published.

## Still needed for substantive release

This is a complete proposed allocation of these target provisions, not complete interpretation research. Before lessons relying on them, check relevant recitals and current Commission material; source accessibility scope under both cited directives; read GDPR Article 35 and Law Enforcement Directive Article 27 before teaching a particular DPIA trigger; source any actual sector assessment or national biometric/worker procedure. Verify availability and status before teaching a simplified form, FRIA template, contract model term, common specification or harmonised standard as usable today.

Assessors can learn to identify these lookups and record missing evidence now. They must not receive a fabricated substantive answer where the source or jurisdiction is unknown. German/Czech teaching editions and specialist legal review remain later work.
