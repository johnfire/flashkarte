## Corrections and source checks

The consolidation confirms the extended Article 6 rules, new Article 5 provisions and differing dates. Article 50(6) confirms that transparency obligations can overlap high-risk requirements. Article 4 and other applicable law prevent a blanket “minimal risk = no obligations” teaching rule. Article 2's research exclusions and Article 5's specific regulated actions prevent the grid's “must not be developed” shorthand from becoming an indiscriminate research ban.

For emotion recognition, recital 18 explicitly distinguishes physical fatigue from emotions/intentions. A system that infers emotion from biometrics at work is a different case from detecting driver fatigue to prevent accidents. Cases still need their exact function and context; a label or a “safety” claim is not a universal exemption. Source: original OJ Act, recital 18, alongside current Articles 3(39), 5(1)(f).

The Article 50 guideline publication date, **20 July 2026**, and final Code date, **10 June 2026**, are confirmed on [the official guideline page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems) and [Code page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content). The guideline PDF identifies **C(2026) 5054 final**. Selected paragraphs 11–14, 25–31 and 111–116 were read; the complete guideline and Code still need lesson-specific review. Guideline paragraph 31 supports the agent-disclosure framing, while Article 50(1) remains the binding text. Its paragraph 113 interprets “existing” to include plausible subjects; explicitly distinguish that interpretation from the statutory words.

Article 50(4)'s text exception combines a review/control process with a natural or legal person's editorial responsibility. An internal report, public-interest publication and edited publication are different fact patterns. It is insufficient to conclude “public-interest text always needs a label” without testing the exception, or “no visible label means no machine-readable marking.”

The presentation's GPAI compute and modification thresholds, exact sector-assessment examples and standard-publication status remain reading tasks in the [legal queue](legal-baseline.md). We have not treated them as verified legal conclusions.

## Original case patterns to develop

Each is a fictional teaching prompt, with changed-fact variants. A source and missing facts must be supplied before a definitive answer is authored.

| Case                      | Lessons               | Decision and changed facts                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Company assistant         | I02–I04               | Same model in an employee chat tool, an applicant scorer and a customer agent; change purpose, actor and outputs                                                       |
| Medical image app         | P04–P05               | Identify covered product and required third-party route; contrast a documented medical purpose with a genuinely different non-medical function, not a disclaimer alone |
| Hearing protection        | P02–P05               | AI separates warning signals from harmful noise; contrast safety protection and comfort-only audio processing, including failure effects                               |
| Application pipeline      | A05–A06, E02, E05     | File-format conversion, candidate ranking and interview scheduling; change decision influence and profiling facts                                                      |
| Learning tool             | A03–A06, E01          | Voluntary study advice, grade evaluation and admission decisions; change purpose, effect and user context                                                              |
| Consumer credit           | S02, E06, B05         | Creditworthiness vs fraud detection; contrast individual crime predictions based solely on personality with fact-based support for human assessment                    |
| Workspace camera          | A02, B08              | Biometric emotion inference vs physical fatigue detection; identify definition, setting and exact medical/safety boundary                                              |
| Generative media supplier | B10–B12               | Intended output, foreseeable reproducible misuse and safeguards; change provider/deployer purpose and placement date; no graphic media                                 |
| Station identification    | R01–R06               | Target objective, public space, law-enforcement purpose, urgency, authorisation refusal and use of output; change one fact at a time                                   |
| Meeting avatar            | T02–T04, T10          | Direct human interaction and realistic synthetic representation; contrast disclosed fiction with a representation appearing authentic                                  |
| Layered procurement       | T05–T08               | API supplier → platform → edited export; ask which marking evidence survives and who has each obligation                                                               |
| Fictional advertisement   | T10                   | Realistic generated employees or a furnished property; change how the audience understands authenticity and how disclosure is presented                                |
| Report publication        | T11–T12               | Internal email → public environmental report → reviewed publication with editorial responsibility; test every condition                                                |
| Model replacement         | I03, later Article 25 | Same documented purpose with a new model versus changed capability/function; ask what must be reassessed and who bears responsibility                                  |

Keep the cases short enough to isolate a legal choice. Detailed sector law, national procedure, technical provenance and difficult interpretations get their own lessons; they are not hidden inside an introductory question.
