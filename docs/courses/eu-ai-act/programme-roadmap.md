# Programme after the first instalment

Chris approved Article 50 as the next dedicated block on 6 October 2026. The first release remains introduction → Article 6 → Article 5, with 52 lessons. The full Act needs several linked course parts; coverage in one part must not be mistaken for coverage of the entire regulation.

## Next: Article 50 — interaction and content transparency

Proposed **12 lessons in three modules**, English first, then German and Czech. These are lesson-boundary proposals, not an imported concept graph. Apply the same source and prerequisite review before authoring. Cross-course prior knowledge must be taught or explicitly supported by the platform; do not assume enrolment in the first part.

| ID  | Practical question                                                              | Source target                                                                                                   |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| T01 | Which actor, system and output are we assessing?                                | Articles 2, 3, 50; guideline role/scope paragraphs; model/system refresher                                      |
| T02 | When must a person know they are interacting with AI?                           | Article 50(1); intended direct interaction, context and obviousness                                             |
| T03 | What should an agent disclose to recipients and its supervising person?         | Article 50(1); guideline paragraph 31; distinguish statutory text from its interpretation                       |
| T04 | Can we design an accessible disclosure at the right moment?                     | Articles 50(5), 50(6); interaction capstone, overlapping high-risk duties and prohibitions                      |
| T05 | Which synthetic outputs need machine-readable marking?                          | Article 50(2); audio, image, video, text; assistive-editing and law-enforcement exceptions                      |
| T06 | What makes marking technically effective, interoperable, robust and reliable?   | Article 50(2); technical feasibility, cost, state of the art; final Code Section 1                              |
| T07 | What evidence should procurement and self-hosting supply?                       | Actor allocation, guideline supply-chain discussion, Code Section 1; Article 111(4) transition                  |
| T08 | Can we test whether provenance survives our output workflow?                    | Marking/detection capstone; export, transformation, validation and documented limitations                       |
| T09 | When must people be told about emotion recognition or biometric categorisation? | Article 50(3); data-protection relationship; Article 5 precheck                                                 |
| T10 | What qualifies as a deepfake, and how is it disclosed?                          | Articles 3(60), 50(4); guideline paragraphs 113–116; artistic/creative/satirical treatment                      |
| T11 | When does public-interest text need a disclosure or an editorial process?       | Article 50(4), second subparagraph; human review OR editorial control AND editorial responsibility              |
| T12 | Can we defend a complete publication decision?                                  | Articles 50(1)–(7); clear timing/accessibility, overlapping duties, guideline vs voluntary Code, final capstone |

T06–T08 will teach what a method demonstrates and where it fails, not promise that a watermark, visible icon or supplier statement alone establishes compliance. Visible labels and machine-readable marking are different obligations. A label cannot legitimise a prohibited use. Add lessons if the reviewed atomic concept graph cannot fit these boundaries; do not compress missing rules to preserve an arbitrary count.
