# Legal baseline

Checked: 7 October 2026, against EUR-Lex. Sources and hashes are in the [source register](source-register.md).

## What the course teaches

The **GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as in force on 7 October 2026**.

- Published in the Official Journal, L 119, on 4 May 2016. It entered into force on the twentieth day after
  publication and has applied since **25 May 2018** (Article 99).
- **No amending act.** EUR-Lex's "Modified by" table for 32016R0679 is empty. Three corrigenda are listed: R(01),
  R(02) and R(03). The working text is the EUR-Lex consolidation `02016R0679-20160504`, which incorporates them.
  Recitals are read from the Official Journal text, because consolidations omit them.
- **Two proposals are pending, not law:**
  - COM(2025) 501 of 21 May 2025, on simplification for small mid-cap enterprises.
  - COM(2025) 837 of 19 November 2025, the "Digital Omnibus". It also proposes changes to the ePrivacy Directive.

  EUR-Lex lists both as "Amendment proposed by" only. Secondary reporting, which is not authoritative, says the data
  part of the Digital Omnibus was still under negotiation in September 2026. Lesson N05 teaches only that these are
  proposals. It says nothing about their content, because that content has not been studied and may change.

## Neighbouring instruments (extension lessons only)

- **ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC, Article 5(3)**, on cookies and terminal equipment. It is a directive, so it
  applies through national law. National laws are deliberately out of scope; the German TDDDG is excluded by Chris's
  decision.
- **EU AI Act, Article 2(7)**, from the 27 July 2026 consolidation: the AI Act does not affect the GDPR, except as
  stated in its Articles 4a and 59.
- **EU–US Data Privacy Framework**, Decision (EU) 2023/1795 of 10 July 2023. EUR-Lex shows it **in force** on 7
  October 2026, with no end date. It shows the decision "Confirmed by" General Court case T-553/23, and a referral
  to the Court of Justice, C-804/25, listed under "Preliminary question submitted by". The course states only these
  facts and tells learners to check the current status before relying on it.

## Case law used

The court cases are used for well-settled points only, and each is quoted from its operative part:

- C-673/17 *Planet49*: pre-ticked cookie boxes.
- C-40/17 *Fashion ID*: joint control through a website plug-in. It was decided under Directive 95/46/EC, and the
  lesson says so.
- C-311/18 *Schrems II*: transfers under standard clauses; Privacy Shield invalid.

## Out of scope by decision (7 October 2026)

- **National law.** This includes the German BDSG and TDDDG. Chris plans two small separate courses for them. The
  course states where the GDPR lets member states add rules, for example Articles 6(2), 8(1), 9(4), 37(4) and 84,
  without teaching any national rule.
- **EDPB guidelines.** None are quoted. Where a lesson relies on interpretation, it uses the article text and
  recitals only. Adding EDPB guidance is a candidate improvement for a later revision.

## Review status

This course is an **educational draft**. No legal professional has reviewed it. Every lesson's first source is the
article it teaches, so a reviewer can check each claim against the text.
